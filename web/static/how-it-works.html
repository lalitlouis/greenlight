How it works

A studio clearance department, run by agents

Before a film shoots, its screenplay goes through clearance: separate desks read the same script looking for different ways it will cost money or fail to release. GREENLIGHT runs that process the way a studio does — four independent desks, then a producer who reconciles them — in about five minutes.

The one non-negotiable rule: a finding without a citation does not appear in your report. Anyone can ask an AI "what's risky in this script" and get a confident paragraph. GREENLIGHT returns claims you can hand to a lawyer — each backed by verbatim, sourced text, each re-checked by an independent verifier before it reaches you.
1

Parse & triage

Deterministic parser + a fast reading pass

Your Fountain screenplay is split into scenes with exact page anchors — no AI involved, so scene references are always right. Then a triage pass extracts every clearance-relevant entity: brands, songs, real people, artworks, film clips, organizations, stunts, and sensitive beats — and writes a worklist for each desk.

2

Clearance Counsel

Rights & clearances desk

Researches every entity on the live web: does this use need a license, from whom, and what does it cost? Music gets the full chase — composition and master recording are separately owned, and the desk follows both chains to the current rightsholder.

3

Ratings Board

MPA rating prediction

Counts the rating drivers in your script — language, violence, drug use — then finds the released films whose content sits nearest to yours in a corpus of 2,500 titles. The prediction is evidence: "seven of your eight nearest comparables are rated R." If you're targeting a lower rating, you get the exact cut list that buys it.

4

Safety Underwriter

Physical production risk

Reads your script like a completion-bond underwriter: stunts, fire, water, night work, minors, animals, weapons — and how they stack. A boat fire is one risk; a boat fire at night with a child in an adjacent skiff is a different, much larger one. Each hazard gets the specialists, permits, and rule-of-thumb costs an insurer will require.

5

Territory Censor

US · UK · China · UAE

What gets cut or blocks release, market by market, under each territory's actual rules — with an honest account of the creative cost when a required cut would touch the heart of your film.

6

Verification & adjudication

The quality gate

Every filed finding goes to an independent verifier that reads only the claim, the scenes it cites, and its sources — never the desk's reasoning. If the source doesn't support the claim, the finding is rejected, and your report shows the rejection and the reason. Then an adjudicator merges duplicates and resolves conflicting remedies, like a producer reconciling the panel.

What you get

  • A Greenlight Score (0–100) — a deterministic function of finding severities, never an AI's opinion.
  • Severity-ranked findings — each with verbatim citations, a concrete remedy ("rename to a fictional brand", "engage a marine coordinator"), and a cost range.
  • A rating prediction with comparables — and the beats to cut for your target.
  • A marked-up script — every finding anchored to its scene, both directions.
  • Honest unknowns — what the desks could not resolve is listed as open questions, not guessed at.
