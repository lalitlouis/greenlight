Methodology

Numbers, not adjectives

Every claim on this page is labeled measured, preliminary, or pending — and the pending ones stay pending until the measurement exists. Updated August 28, 2026.

Rating prediction — measured

We harvested the official CARA rating rationale for 4,755 films from the MPA's own register (88% of our post-1990 corpus; the practice barely existed before 1990, so our scope is post-1990 wide releases, stated plainly). Parsed into intensity-category descriptors, they train a transparent model whose predictions carry a distribution-free coverage guarantee (Mondrian split conformal):

held-out films: 927 (deterministic split, never trained on)
conformal coverage: 92.0% against a 90% target — the guarantee holds
top-1 accuracy: 84.4% · multiclass Brier: 0.245
prediction sets: mostly single ratings; boundary scripts get honest pairs like {PG-13, R}

Two findings from the same data: the R-line for language is the modifier, not the category — "pervasive language" lands R 99% of the time (n=187) while "strong language" lands PG-13 89% (n=383) — and the descriptor→rating mapping is stable across decades: a model trained only on pre-2010 films loses just 1.1 points of accuracy on post-2015 films. The vocabulary drifted; the line didn't.

The guarantee is conditional on descriptor extraction from your script, which is model judgment. We say that plainly rather than hiding it in a footnote.

Cut guidance — grounded, validation growing

The cut list ("these changes buy the lower rating") is checked against a second regulator's paper trail: the BBFC publishes, per film, which cuts were made and what category they achieved. Our harvest holds 436 such records — including 57 that state the full trade ("uncut 15 was available; cuts achieved 12A") — the exact transaction our cut simulator predicts, documented by the regulator that watched it happen.

Run-to-run consistency — preliminary

Same script, same answer? We measure it by running the entire pipeline multiple times and matching findings across passes. On our test screenplay: judgment agreement α = 0.93 — when two passes examine the same entity, they agree on flag-vs-clear 11 times in 12. What varies between passes is coverage (which entities get examined), which is why a deterministic pre-pass now feeds every desk the same candidate floor. Preliminary because the sample is small; the feature-length measurement is built and scheduled, and this label changes only when it runs.

Recall — pending, and we will not fake it

What fraction of findable issues do we find? Honest answer today: we do not publish a recall number yet. The estimator (capture–recapture across independent passes) is built, but it is only honest after validation against independently hand-labeled scripts — and its known biases point optimistic, so when it ships it ships as an upper bound ("no better than X%"), never a point estimate.

Verification — measured per run

Every finding faces a blinded verifier — claim and citations only, never the desk's reasoning — and rejections are shown on every report with the verifier's reason. Across recent runs the verifier rejects roughly 10–30% of draft findings. A finding rejected for citation quality (rather than substance) now gets exactly one re-sourcing round with fresh evidence and a second blinded verdict; each report carries its attempted/recovered counts.

What we do not claim

  • This is a research pre-screen, not a legal opinion, and it does not satisfy E&O requirements — production counsel reviews before production relies.
  • No accuracy number appears here before its measurement exists. When a label above says pending, that is the whole claim.
