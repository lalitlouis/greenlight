Methodology

Numbers, not adjectives

Every claim on this page is labeled measured, preliminary, or pending — and the pending ones stay pending until the measurement exists. Updated August 28, 2026.

Rating prediction — measured

We harvested the official CARA rating rationale for 4,755 films from the MPA's own register (88% of our post-1990 corpus; the practice barely existed before 1990, so our scope is post-1990 wide releases, stated plainly). Parsed into intensity-category descriptors, they train a transparent model whose predictions carry a distribution-free coverage guarantee (Mondrian split conformal):

held-out films: 937 (deterministic split, never trained on)
conformal coverage: 92.0% against a 90% target — the guarantee holds
top-1 accuracy: 83.9% · multiclass Brier: 0.239
prediction sets: mostly single ratings; boundary scripts get honest pairs like {PG-13, R}

The reliability diagram above is the unflattering version of that table, published on purpose. Dot size is bin population (71% of holdout films land in the 0.9–1.0 bin, where confidence and accuracy agree at 93%). In the thin middle bins the raw probability runs overconfident — a 0.74 answer is right about 54% of the time — which is exactly why a report never ships the raw probability as the claim: the conformal set is the guarantee, and it widens to an honest pair precisely where these dots sag below the diagonal.

Two findings from the same data: the R-line for language is the modifier, not the category — "pervasive language" lands R 99% of the time (n=187) while "strong language" lands PG-13 89% (n=384) — and the descriptor→rating mapping drifts measurably across decades: a model trained only on pre-2010 films loses 5.0 points of accuracy on post-2015 films (an earlier, narrower descriptor vocabulary measured 1.1 — the richer vocabulary sees drift the narrow one was blind to). The model you get is trained on the full corpus; recency weighting is a candidate improvement, and if we ship it, this number is the reason.

The population boundary: the guarantee is calibrated on post-1990 US wide-release features with published CARA rationales, and it is a guarantee about that population. A documentary short, an unrated festival cut, or a pre-1990 sensibility sits outside it — the prediction still renders, but the 90% number does not travel with it.

Cut guidance — grounded, validation growing

The cut list ("these changes buy the lower rating") is checked against a second regulator's paper trail: the BBFC publishes, per film, which cuts were made and what category they achieved. Our harvest holds 436 such records — including 57 that state the full trade ("uncut 15 was available; cuts achieved 12A") — the exact transaction our cut simulator predicts, documented by the regulator that watched it happen.

Descriptor extraction — pending, and it is the load-bearing wall

Everything above measures the decision layer: given descriptors, the boundary is measured and the coverage guarantee holds. But the descriptors come from a model reading your script, and that extraction step is not yet measured. If the desk reads your drug content as "some" where CARA would say "pervasive", the 92% coverage does not save the prediction — the guarantee is conditional on correct extraction, and we say that here in plain words rather than in a footnote. The planned measurement is validation against hand-labeled scripts; until it runs, "our model is calibrated" is the claim we make, and "our reports are accurate" is the claim we do not.

Run-to-run consistency — measured, and the news is mixed

Same script, same answer? We measure it by running the entire pipeline multiple times and matching findings across passes — and we publish what came out, not what we hoped. Judgment is consistent: when two passes examine the same item they agree flag-vs-clear 36 times in 37 on the feature-length measurement — raw agreement 97%, Gwet's AC1 = 0.97 (α = 0.93 on the short fixture; at feature scale Krippendorff's alpha degenerates because ~95% of co-examined items are cleared, leaving no variance for the chance correction — AC1 is the chance-corrected statistic built for exactly that regime, and we report all three numbers plus the prevalence so you can see why). Coverage is not yet consistent: across two complete feature-length passes, only 5 of the 16 distinct findings were surfaced by both — different passes chase different threads. And one pass in three had two desks return no dispositions at all, a failure the pipeline now refuses to grade or ship (it renders as an INCOMPLETE banner instead of silence). Coverage convergence is the current engineering target; this section will carry the numbers as they move.

Recall — pending, and we will not fake it

What fraction of findable issues do we find? Honest answer today: we do not publish a recall number yet. The estimator (capture–recapture across independent passes) is built, but it is only honest after validation against independently hand-labeled scripts — and its known biases point optimistic, so when it ships it ships as an upper bound ("no better than X%"), never a point estimate.

Verification — measured per run

Every finding faces a blinded verifier — claim and citations only, never the desk's reasoning — and rejections are shown on every report with the verifier's reason. Across recent runs the verifier rejects roughly 10–30% of draft findings. A finding rejected for citation quality (rather than substance) now gets exactly one re-sourcing round with fresh evidence and a second blinded verdict; each report carries its attempted/recovered counts. The aggregate recovery rate will be published here once enough production runs exist for it to be a statistic rather than an anecdote — until then it stays per-report.

What we do not claim

  • This is a research pre-screen, not a legal opinion, and it does not satisfy E&O requirements — production counsel reviews before production relies.
  • No accuracy number appears here before its measurement exists. When a label above says pending, that is the whole claim.
